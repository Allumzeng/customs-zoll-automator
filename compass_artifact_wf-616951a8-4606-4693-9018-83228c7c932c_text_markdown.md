# Output Formats & Integration Patterns for a German Mittelstand Customs/Zoll Document Automator

## TL;DR
- **Build the MVP as a "pre-system" that outputs structured JSON over a webhook/REST API plus a column-mappable CSV/Excel file — not EDIFACT or ATLAS XML directly.** The dominant deployed pattern in German customs AI (Digicust, DAKOSY Drag&Map) is to extract and validate data, then hand a clean payload to the customer's *existing* certified customs software (ATLAS participant software like DAKOSY ZODIAK, AEB, Format, or Riege Scope), which performs the legally-binding ATLAS submission. You should not try to file to ATLAS yourself.
- **Do not build direct ATLAS connectivity in 3–4 weeks.** Speaking to German customs requires a fully-programmed, government-certified participant application (Teilnehmersoftware) — German customs is explicit that "the mere exchange of EDIFACT or XML messages does not lead to the issuing of a certificate." This is months of work and a regulatory moat owned by incumbents. Your wedge is the document→structured-data layer feeding those incumbents.
- **Ship a human-in-the-loop review screen with field-level confidence scores as a first-class feature, not an afterthought.** Under EU UCC Art. 15(2) the declarant remains fully liable for accuracy regardless of the tool used, so "deployable for real cargo" means a forwarder can see, verify and correct every field before it flows into their ATLAS software. The minimum credible output is: validated structured data + per-field confidence + source-document provenance + a one-click export into the format their customs system ingests.

## Key Findings

### 1. There is no single "output format" — there is a layered stack, and the winning layer for a solo build is the data-handoff layer
German customs data moves through a layered chain: commercial documents (PDF/email) → structured extraction → the forwarder's TMS/customs software → ATLAS (the government system). The question "what format should I output" resolves to "what does the forwarder's customs software ingest," and the answer across vendors is remarkably consistent: **structured data via API/file (JSON, XML, CSV/Excel), mapped to that system's field schema.**

### 2. ATLAS itself is XML-based (formerly EDIFACT), but you cannot and should not target it directly
ATLAS (Automatisiertes Tarif- und lokales Zoll-Abwicklungssystem) is the German customs authority's electronic clearance system. It accepts messages in **XML (formerly EDIFACT)**. Participation requires an EORI number, a Beteiligten-Identifikationsnummer (BIN), and either certified participant software or the free web portal (IAA Plus / the new Zoll-Portal). Crucially, German customs requires **certification of participant software** — and "the mere exchange of EDIFACT or XML messages does not lead to the issuing of a certificate"; you need a fully-programmed, real-operation-capable application that passes a two-part test (automated ZefA test + business-scenario test). The current production release is **ATLAS Release 10.2, per zoll.de in Echtbetrieb since 28 Feb 2026 (delayed from 15 Nov 2025), adding "Zentrale Zollabwicklung in Einfuhrverfahren (Centralised Clearance for Import, CCI)"; first maintenance window WF 01 set for 25 April 2026.** This certification regime is precisely why incumbents (DAKOSY, AEB, Format, Riege Scope, CargoSoft) own the filing layer and why AI startups position as pre-systems.

### 3. EDIFACT message types relevant to freight (context, not your output target)
UN/EDIFACT is the dominant EDI standard in European logistics. The freight-relevant message types are: **CUSDEC** (customs declaration), **CUSCAR** (customs cargo report), **CUSRES** (customs response), **IFTMIN** (forwarding/transport instruction — the classic shipping order), **IFCSUM** (consolidated forwarding), **IFTSTA** (status report), and **INVOIC** (invoice). In Germany's domestic groupage (Sammelgut) world, the **FORTRAS** standard (BORD/BORDERO ≈ IFTMIN, ENTL ≈ RECADV, STAT ≈ IFTSTA; fixed-length record format, current release BORD512) remains the de-facto interchange standard between forwarder TMSs. These are relevant to understand the ecosystem, but they are heavyweight, partner-specific, and the wrong first output for an MVP.

### 4. ZUGFeRD / XRechnung are for *invoicing*, not customs declaration output — mostly out of scope
ZUGFeRD (hybrid PDF+XML, B2B) and XRechnung (pure XML, B2G mandatory) are EN 16931 e-invoicing formats driven by Germany's e-invoicing mandate (since 1 Jan 2025 all German businesses must be able to *receive* structured e-invoices; B2B issuance phases in toward 2028). They are relevant in two narrow ways: (a) a commercial invoice *arriving* as ZUGFeRD gives you a clean embedded XML payload to extract from instead of OCR'ing a PDF; (b) the forwarder's own billing of customs services may use ZUGFeRD/XRechnung. They are **not** a customs-declaration output format and should not be your primary target. Per a representative survey of exactly 1,103 German companies with 20+ employees, conducted by Bitkom Research (telephone, calendar weeks 16–23 2024): among e-invoice users, "Knapp drei Viertel der Unternehmen nutzen diese (71 Prozent)" EDI formats, "ZUGFeRD bzw. Factur-X (27 Prozent)" and "XRechnung (5 Prozent)" — i.e. hybrid and pure-XML formats are a minority of usage, reinforcing that this is an invoicing concern, not a customs-output one.

### 5. The German Mittelstand TMS/customs-software landscape
- **CargoWise (WiseTech)** — global market leader at the top end; over-engineered and expensive for SMB; recent price hikes pushing forwarders to evaluate alternatives.
- **Riege Scope** — "the European answer to CargoWise," dominant among DACH freight forwarders, strong native ATLAS/AES/NCTS customs integration. Customers report meaningful processing-time savings on customs declarations. Exposes a **Scope Customs Integration (SCI)** interface and REST APIs; accepts orders from ERP/TMS and can ingest CSV with saveable field mappings. ~550 customers, 6,000+ users.
- **CargoSoft** — German Bremen-based TMS, very common in German SMB forwarding. Exposes a **REST API using JSON** with token auth; the CargoCom/EDI module supports 150+ partner interfaces in **XML, JSON, EDIFACT, Excel, CSV**, plus its own CargoSoftFile format and standard interfaces to DAKOSY, Fortras 100 and DATEV.
- **DAKOSY (ZODIAK GE)** — major German seaport/customs software (DAKOSY, founded 1982, Hamburg, ~220 employees), which per Digicust "operates the Port Community System for the Port of Hamburg… serving over 3,000 customers." Its **BOX (Business Objects Exchange) interface** ingests data from in-house TMS/ERP, and its **Drag&Map** tool maps **Excel/CSV** columns to declaration fields. Digicust integrates with DAKOSY via **SFTP** (Zodiak Box).
- **AEB, Format (FORMAT-IVEAS), dbh, BEO, ZOBA** — other certified German customs software vendors, all positioned as ATLAS gateways with SAP/ERP add-ons and APIs.
- Smaller SMB road/groupage TMS: CarLo (Soloplan), WinSped, LIS, Logistiqo, M3 Logisticware, SE4L.

**The "lowest common denominator" finding:** every one of these systems can ingest **CSV/Excel and XML**, and the modern ones expose **REST/JSON APIs**. DAKOSY and Riege both ship explicit Excel/CSV column-mapping tools precisely because that is what their SMB customers can produce. CargoSoft's own EDI lead frames XML/JSON/EDIFACT/Excel/CSV as the interface palette.

### 6. Real-world AI customs automation output patterns — there IS a converging de-facto pattern
The clearest reference is **Digicust** (Austrian/German, customs-AI since 2020, TÜV SÜD / IEEE CertifAIed, ISO 27001), which is the closest analog to the proposed tool and explicitly serves German Mittelstand forwarders (Wackler, Rüdinger). Its architecture is instructive:
- **Ingest:** email (per-strategy ingest address), drag-and-drop, API; formats PDF, Office, images, .eml/.msg, and structured CSV/JSON/XML/YAML.
- **Output / handoff:** "submission-ready data" pushed into the customer's existing system — **ATLAS, AES, NCTS, DAKOSY, AEB, Format, SCOPE, Mercurio, dbh, e2open** — via **REST API, webhooks (with base64 attachments), SFTP (pre-built profiles for SCOPE, DAKOSY, E2OPEN, MERCURIO, DBH), email, and MCP.** Its webhook payload is plain structured JSON (invoiceNumber, items[] with hsCode/value, etc.), optionally transformed by a "mapping" into the downstream schema.
- **Explicit positioning:** "We push validated declarations into the system you already trust. We do not replace it." "No technical integration required — Digicust works as a pre-system."
- Other tools confirm the pattern: Affinda/DeepOpinion/Customaite/Reform/ABBYY all output **structured JSON / mapped fields via REST API + webhooks + human review UI**, integrating with TMS/ERP/customs systems rather than filing directly. The independent FreightMynd 2026 guide states bluntly: "The most important evaluation criterion for any AI tool is TMS integration — if it cannot push clean data into your system of record, it creates new problems."

**De-facto standard for AI-extracted logistics data: structured JSON over REST/webhook, plus a file-based fallback (CSV/Excel/XML over SFTP/email) for partners who "can only speak files," with a configurable field-mapping layer to hit each downstream system's schema.**

### 7. What forwarders actually demand when evaluating customs AI ("must-have" vs "nice-to-have")
- **Must-have:** (1) clean data that lands in their existing customs/TMS system in the exact fields they need ("we get the data in the fields where we need it" — Rüdinger); (2) a human verification step before submission; (3) handling of their messy, supplier-specific document formats and mixed multi-document PDFs; (4) field-level provenance/auditability.
- **Nice-to-have:** AI HS-code classification with reasoning, multi-document conflict detection, natural-language correction, sanctions/export-control screening, multi-country coverage.
- The build-vs-buy literature is consistent that **document processing is where custom builds beat generic SaaS** because edge cases (unusual supplier formats, specific TMS field mappings) determine whether the tool works in production vs. only in a demo.

### 8. Human-in-the-loop: the legal and accuracy reality
- **Legal liability sits with the declarant (Anmelder), always.** EU UCC Art. 15(2) makes the person lodging the declaration "responsible for … the accuracy and completeness of the information given," and binds a customs representative to the same obligation. The declarant is the legal "person" (UCC Art. 5); an AI tool cannot be the responsible party. The customs debt is incurred the moment the declaration is accepted (Art. 77), so accuracy *before* submission is what matters.
- **German penalties make human oversight non-negotiable:** under §378 Abgabenordnung, even *reckless* (leichtfertig, i.e. gross-negligence) incorrect declarations are an administrative offence punishable by up to €50,000, per §378(2) AO verbatim: "Die Ordnungswidrigkeit kann mit einer Geldbuße bis zu fünfzigtausend Euro geahndet werden." §370 AO (intentional evasion) carries up to 5 years' imprisonment (up to 10 in serious cases). Blindly trusting AI output without review risks being treated as leichtfertig. German legal commentary stresses the declarant's Erkundigungspflicht (duty to verify) — outsourcing to a forwarder or software does *not* transfer responsibility.
- **No regulator mandates a specific "human must click approve" rule** for AI declarations — the requirement is *indirect*, flowing from liability. But the industry treats human-in-the-loop review of low-confidence fields as standard practice. Digicust itself states the aim is "not to eliminate review, but to focus it where it matters most," keeping people involved "where … legal responsibility is required."
- **Confidence-score routing is the universal pattern:** each extracted field gets a confidence score; high-confidence fields auto-populate, low-confidence fields route to a review queue showing the value alongside the source-document region; corrections become training data. This is implemented by Digicust, Extend, Box, LlamaParse, ABBYY, and is the design AWS/industry guides recommend.
- **Accuracy numbers are vendor marketing, not regulatory thresholds.** Digicust advertises ~98% document accuracy (and ~95% for tariff classification "when goods descriptions are complete"), explicitly labelling these as vendor-stated figures dependent on document quality. ABBYY/CustomsTrack advertises "100% accuracy and a 99% straight-through-processing rate" for a single UK food-importer case — a marketing claim, in tension with the legal reality that "a customs declaration is a legal document [with] no margin for error." There is **no published accuracy threshold above which auto-submission is legally permitted**; the safe design keeps a human gate regardless of confidence.
- **Demonstrated ROI in the Mittelstand:** Per Digicust's Wackler case study (IT head Marc Fiegert), Wackler Spedition & Logistik (175-year family forwarder, CargoLine member) automated 2,000 export + 14,000 transit declarations = 16,000 in a 3-month run, cutting T-paper processing "39 working hours per week to just 14 hours per week" (64% cut), achieved in the first 7 weeks across two sites, feeding DAKOSY. Rüdinger Spedition (96-year-old forwarder, 700 staff), per Digicust's 23 Feb 2026 case study with Head of Customs Martina Kainz: "From document upload to employee review, the entire process now takes only about 5 minutes," with data flowing into DAKOSY and staff merely verifying pre-filled fields. ZLS Logistik reported a 90% processing-time reduction.

## Details: Recommended MVP Output Stack

**Primary output (build first): structured JSON via webhook/REST, with a documented schema.**
- One canonical JSON object per document set (shipment/case), with header-level fields (parties with EORI, Incoterms, currency, invoice number/value, country of origin, transport details) and a line-items array (description, HS/Warennummer, quantity, weight, value, packaging). Model it on the EU Customs Data Model (EUCDM) / UCC Annex B H1 (import) and AES 3.0 export data elements so your fields map cleanly to ATLAS later. Include a per-field `confidence` and `source` (document + bounding region) for every value.
- Deliver via outbound webhook (POST, with the source PDF as base64 or a link) and a polling GET endpoint. This is exactly Digicust's pattern and what modern TMS (CargoSoft, Riege) consume natively.

**Secondary output (build alongside): a column-mappable CSV/Excel export + SFTP drop.**
- This is the genuine lowest-common-denominator. DAKOSY's Drag&Map and Riege Scope's customs interface both ingest Excel/CSV with saveable per-customer column mappings; CargoSoft ingests CSV/Excel. A clean, consistent CSV/XLSX (header sheet + line-items sheet) plus the ability to SFTP it to a watched folder makes you immediately usable by forwarders whose customs software can't take an API push.
- Make the column order/headers configurable per customer (a one-time mapping config), because each downstream profile differs.

**Do NOT build for the MVP:** direct ATLAS XML filing, EDIFACT CUSDEC generation, or ATLAS participant certification. These are months-long, certification-gated, and already owned by DAKOSY/AEB/Format/Riege. If a client later wants "straight into DAKOSY," replicate Digicust's approach: SFTP a mapped file into the DAKOSY Zodiak BOX (the interface DAKOSY sets up), or hand off via the partner's documented interface.

**Mandatory MVP feature — the review screen:** a field-level verification UI showing extracted value, confidence, and the highlighted source-document snippet, with an exception queue for low-confidence/conflicting fields and an explicit human "approve & export" action. This is what converts "demo" into "deployable for real cargo," and it is your legal-liability shield for the forwarder.

**Minimum viable output a forwarder needs to consider it deployable:**
1. All declaration-relevant fields extracted and validated, mapped to the fields their customs system expects;
2. Per-field confidence + source provenance + audit trail;
3. A human review/correction step before anything leaves the tool;
4. One-click handoff in *their* ingest format — JSON/API if modern, CSV/Excel/XML-over-SFTP if not.

## Recommendations (staged)

**Stage 0 — Pick one design partner and one downstream target (Week 0).** Find a single German SMB forwarder and ask exactly one question: "What customs/TMS software do you file ATLAS with, and what can it import?" If it's DAKOSY → target Excel/CSV (Drag&Map) + SFTP BOX handoff. If Riege Scope → target its CSV customs interface / SCI. If CargoSoft → target its REST/JSON API. Build to that one target first. **Benchmark that changes the plan:** if your design partner has no importable customs system and files only via the free IAA Plus web portal, your output target becomes a clean, copy-paste-ready review screen + Excel, not an API.

**Stage 1 — MVP (Weeks 1–4):** Email ingest → Claude extraction → canonical JSON with confidence + provenance → human review screen → export as (a) JSON via webhook and (b) mapped CSV/Excel. Ship the review UI as the centerpiece. Model the JSON schema on EUCDM/UCC Annex B field names so it's ATLAS-mappable.

**Stage 2 — First integration hardening (Months 2–3):** Add SFTP delivery with per-customer field mapping; add multi-document conflict detection (invoice vs. packing list weight/value cross-check — the exact discrepancy customs officers hunt for); add HS-code suggestion with confidence (clearly flagged as suggestion, never auto-final).

**Stage 3 — Deeper handoff (only if pulled by paying customers):** Build a tested file/SFTP handoff into the specific downstream system the customer uses (DAKOSY BOX, Riege SCI), replicating the Digicust pattern of "validated data into the system you already trust." Consider EDIFACT/ATLAS-XML generation only if a customer's system specifically requires it and pays for it.

**Thresholds that should change your strategy:**
- If multiple early customers all use the *same* downstream system, invest in a deep native connector for it rather than breadth.
- If a customer demands you file to ATLAS directly, treat it as a different (much larger, certification-gated) product and either partner with an existing certified vendor or decline for the MVP.
- If document variety is overwhelming extraction accuracy, narrow to one or two document types (commercial invoice + packing list) before adding CMR/AWB/B-L.

## Caveats
- **Vendor accuracy figures (98%/99%/100% STP) are marketing claims**, not independently audited and not regulatory permissions to skip human review; several of the strongest claims come from single-customer case studies and non-German (UK CDS) contexts. Treat them as aspirational, not as a design spec.
- **No official German/EU rule explicitly requires human approval of AI-generated declarations** — the requirement is inferred from declarant liability (UCC Art. 15/77; §§370/378 AO). The EU AI Act's treatment of customs AI as "high-risk" is not clearly settled. If precise legal positioning matters to a client, recommend they confirm with their own customs counsel / the Generalzolldirektion.
- **ATLAS is a moving target:** release 10.2 went live 28 Feb 2026 (adding Centralised Clearance/CCI); AES 3.0 added mandatory fields (carrier, means-of-transport registration, country of origin, security indicator, LRN→MRN). Any field schema you build must be versioned and maintainable.
- The TMS market-share specifics for the German SMB segment are drawn from vendor and trade-press sources rather than a formal market survey; treat the relative ranking (Scope/CargoSoft/DAKOSY strong in DACH SMB; CargoWise strong at the top end) as directionally reliable rather than precise.
- This analysis assumes the goal is a pre-system feeding existing customs software. If the actual ambition is to become a certified ATLAS filer, the timeline, cost and regulatory burden are an order of magnitude larger and out of scope for a 3–4 week solo MVP.
